Luis A. Hernández Gómez is Associate Professor at the Escuela Técnica Superior de Ingenieros de Telecomunicación, Universidad Politécnica de Madrid (ETSIT-UPM). He received his Telecommunications Engineering degree in 1982 and his PhD in Telecommunications Engineering in 1988, both from UPM. His distinctions include the Award of the Spanish Official Association of Telecommunications Engineers and UPM's Extraordinary Doctoral Award. Since 2016, he has coordinated the Signal Processing and Machine Learning for Big Data specialization within the Master's Degree in Signal Theory and Communications.

His research focuses on signal processing, machine learning, deep learning, and artificial intelligence applied to speech, audio, language, and conversational technologies. His work includes speech coding, text-to-speech synthesis, automatic speech and speaker recognition, emotion recognition, and spoken dialogue systems, including research associated with the Alexa Prize. He also investigates generative and agentic AI and, through the EU ASTOUND project, artificial consciousness and socially aware virtual agents. A major research line concerns the assessment of medical conditions from speech and language, including sleep apnoea, cognitive impairment, and suicide risk. His recent research on suicide-risk assessment combines open-discourse semantic analysis with prosodic, acoustic, and paralinguistic evidence. He has also developed AI methods for human activity recognition and driver-behavior characterization using data acquired from smartphone sensors, wearable devices, and embedded systems.

He has maintained a long-term research and technology-transfer collaboration with Telefónica. This collaboration covered speech technologies, AI-based driver characterization from smartphone sensor data, rural telecommunications planning, and, more recently, Agentic AI for KPIs Radio Access Network (RAN) analysis in Mobile Network Optimization.
 
He has participated in more than 16 competitive research projects, supervised 26 doctoral theses, authored or co-authored 53 JCR-indexed journal papers, contributed to four international patents, and led 36 technology-transfer contracts.
